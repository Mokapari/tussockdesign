A proposal for a year of design research

Resilient homes for Aotearoa New Zealand

I'm proposing a year of design research into how New Zealand homes can stand up to a changing climate. A developer would be my client and mentor. The year ends in a practical handbook for the people who design and build houses here.

How can residential design make New Zealand more resilient?

By resilient I mean three things a house has to do. Keep people safe through the event. Keep working through it: water, power, warmth and food. And be repaired, adapted or moved afterwards. Every idea in the year gets tested against those three.

Survive

Flood, coast, wind, fire and ground, read site by site.

Keep working

Energy, water, waste and food that carry a household through an outage.

Recover

Houses that can be repaired, adapted or moved, and the rules that help.

In short

  • Scope: single homes, in three settings: rural new builds, urban new builds and urban renovations.
  • Shape: about twelve months, about 30 hours a week. I'd run it like an architectural thesis, with reviews and a monthly pin-up.
  • When: April 2027 to March 2028.
  • What comes out of it: the NZ Resilient Design Handbook, a reference for architects, planners, designers and licensed building practitioners. Plus the design moves, and a brief for a second year that designs the house.

Why tussock

Tussock is one of New Zealand's own plants. The tall tussocks, Chionochloa, are found almost nowhere else.

It's built the way I want a house to be. It grows as a tight clump, with its growing parts tucked into the base, so it comes through drought and fire, and grows back after. And tussock country holds water and lets it go slowly, which takes the edge off floods downstream.

It survives, it keeps working, and it recovers. That's the whole idea of this year, in one plant. So it's the name, and it's the drawing beside every page.
