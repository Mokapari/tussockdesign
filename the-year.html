The year

Three semesters and a final month

Twelve months, April 2027 to March 2028: three semesters of three months, breaks between them, and a month at the end to finish the Handbook and present it.

This is the shape I'm proposing, and it's open to your input.

Semester 1: the ground and the weather

The question: what does a New Zealand house have to survive, and where?

I'll map the hazards and climate zones across the country, and study what recent events show: the 2023 Auckland floods and Cyclone Gabrielle, and the Canterbury and Kaikōura earthquakes. Then the codes and standards each hazard has to meet, and the first precedents redrawn.

Ends with the proposal review: a one-page thesis statement, the annotated sources and the hazard maps.

Semester 2: how New Zealand builds and lives

The question: what do New Zealand homes use, make and depend on, and what fails first?

This is the fieldwork semester, into the spring. I'll visit homesteads and off-grid homes, and talk with builders, suppliers and councils. I'll compare materials and systems by cost, carbon, supply and repair, across energy, water, wastewater and food.

Ends with the mid review: the fieldwork record, the comparisons and the first chapters in draft.

Semester 3: the design moves

The question: which design moves answer each hazard, and what do the areas of research teach?

I'll draw the moves as diagrams and set them against the three settings. I'll redraw and compare precedents from each area of research, from traditional Māori and Japanese building to modernism, brutalism, modular building, homesteading and adaptive reuse. By the end, the precedent library and the hazard-and-response matrix are done.

Ends with the pre-final review: the Handbook in draft, the design moves and the matrix.

The final month

I'll finish the Handbook and present it to you and the panel. Then a brief for a second year, if one makes sense, and a handover.

Running through all three

Learning from traditional Māori building and materials, as research, reviewed by a Māori advisor. And the monthly pin-up with you.

Design research first. The first year is design research. Designing the house, a modular base house tested against every hazard in all three settings, would be a second year if it makes sense, and the final month writes its brief. The full scope is more than one year can hold, so food and refuge stay shorter chapters, drawn from published work.
